\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document is the \textbf{Task~3} experimental supplement for the course project: supervised bounding-box localization of zombies from \texttt{rgb\_array} observations in Knights, Archers and Zombies (KAZ) under controlled visual distortion, using the PettingZoo simulator interface.
We compare heatmap regressors, ImageNet-transfer heads, Ultralytics YOLO and RT-DETR, a Faster~R-CNN baseline, and classical detectors; we report held-out test precision stratified by distortion, training dynamics, and manual-feature ablations.
The first part of the project PDF should remain self-contained for Tasks~1--2; detailed learning-curve and ablation plots are deferred to Appendix~A so this section stays short enough to merge under the global 10-page body limit (references excluded).

\section{Introduction and related work}
The assignment requires a vision module that tolerates combined distortions on pixel observations~\citep{mlproject2026}.
Systematic studies of CNN robustness under common image corruptions motivate stress-testing detectors beyond i.i.d.\ pixels~\citep{hendrycks2019benchmarking}.
KAZ is provided in PettingZoo~\citep{terry2021pettingzoo}; observations are generated in code with the parallel API and optional wrappers for distortion (see \texttt{zombie\_detection/generate\_dataset.py}).
Multi-agent deep RL often builds on shared environment APIs and pixel observations~\citep{hernandez2019marl}; PettingZoo documents visual policies for KAZ as a worked example~\citep{pettingzoo_kaz,sb3_kaz_tutorial}.
There is \textbf{no} dedicated peer-reviewed benchmark for ``object detection on KAZ pixels''; we therefore ground detector choices in surveys and canonical architectures~\citep{liu2020deep,lin2014coco,ren2015fasterrcnn,zhao2024rtdetr} and cite PettingZoo / MARL references where the simulator and training recipes are concerned~\citep{terry2021pettingzoo,pettingzoo_kaz}.

\section{Research questions and design choices}
\label{sec:rq}
We phrase engineering decisions as research questions (assignment criterion).

\begin{description}[style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[RQ1 (detector family)] Which detector families maximize test precision on the \texttt{mixed} distortion pool while staying within our training/resolution constraints?
  \item[RQ2 (transfer vs scratch)] When is ImageNet/COCO initialization preferable to training from scratch for small, stylized sprites?
  \item[RQ3 (manual channels)] Do optional handcrafted channels (edges, HSV saturation, LBP) improve heatmap-based detectors under strong distortion, and are they complementary?
  \item[RQ4 (baselines)] How far behind are classical and two-stage baselines relative to modern one-stage and transformer detectors under the same evaluation code?
\end{description}

\noindent\textbf{Answers (supported by Secs.~\ref{sec:dataset}--\ref{sec:results}).}
For RQ1--2, one-stage Ultralytics models and RT-DETR dominate the matrix on \texttt{precision\_best\_mixed} (Table~\ref{tab:bestmixed}), consistent with strong transfer from large-scale detection pretraining~\citep{pan2010transfer,yosinski2014transfer} and with mainstream one-stage / DETR-style designs~\citep{redmon2018yolov3,ultralytics_yolo_docs,zhao2024rtdetr,carion2020detr}.
Heatmap models with manual features remain competitive when Jetson-like memory limits matter (Table~\ref{tab:bestmixed} and Appendix~A).
For RQ3, leave-one-out and single-feature ablations show channels are complementary (Appendix~A, Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_loo}--\ref{fig:ablation_sf}), consistent with classical texture cues (e.g.\ LBP~\citep{ojala2002lbp}) remaining informative under appearance shift when fused with learned representations.
For RQ4, HOG+SVM, template matching, and our Faster~R-CNN configuration yield very low mixed precision in the logged campaign (Table~\ref{tab:bestmixed}), i.e.\ they act as negative controls unless substantially retuned.

\section{Dataset, splits, and simulator protocol}
\label{sec:dataset}
\textbf{Splits.} Train / validation / test follow standard supervised practice: parameters updated on train only; validation monitors loss and precision each epoch; test is held out for distortion-stratified metrics in \texttt{comparison.csv}.

\textbf{Sizes.} From \texttt{config.yaml}: \texttt{num\_train\_examples}${}=8000$, \texttt{num\_val\_examples}${}=1000$, \texttt{num\_test\_examples}${}=1000$ base frames per split; each sample stores an RGB frame and zombie boxes in screen coordinates.

\textbf{Generation.} \texttt{generate\_dataset.py} steps KAZ with PettingZoo~\citep{terry2021pettingzoo}, applies configured distortion levels, and writes \texttt{rgb\_array} at $1280\times720$.
Training loaders (\texttt{train.py}) shuffle train, disable shuffle on validation, and apply stochastic augmentation only on the train branch of \texttt{build\_preprocessing\_pipeline} (standard practice for improving generalization~\citep{shorten2019augmentation}).

\textbf{Resolution paths.} Heatmap / ResNet / Faster~R-CNN consume NumPy packs resized to $360\times640$ in the pipeline; YOLO and RT-DETR use an exported Ultralytics dataset at the same training resolution (\texttt{YOLODetector.export\_dataset\_to\_yolo\_format}).

\section{Evaluation metric (reproducible)}
\label{sec:metric}
Each frame has ground-truth axis-aligned boxes $(l,t,w,h)$ in pixels.
For predicted boxes $\mathcal{P}$ and ground truth $\mathcal{G}$, we use greedy one-to-one matching in prediction order: a prediction matches an unmatched ground-truth box if $\mathrm{IoU}\ge0.5$.
With $\mathrm{IoU}(a,b)=\lvert a\cap b\rvert/\lvert a\cup b\rvert$ and $M$ the number of matched pairs,
\begin{equation}
  \text{precision}_{\mathrm{IoU0.5}} =
  \begin{cases}
    1 & \text{if }|\mathcal{G}|=0\text{ and }|\mathcal{P}|=0,\\
    0 & \text{if }|\mathcal{G}|=0\text{ and }|\mathcal{P}|>0,\\
    M/|\mathcal{G}| & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This matches \texttt{compute\_precision\_iou} in \texttt{zombie\_detection/train.py} (default threshold $0.5$).
The $\mathrm{IoU}\!\ge\!0.5$ cutoff matches the ``PASCAL-style'' overlap rule widely reused in detection benchmarks~\citep{everingham2010pascal,lin2014coco}; our scalar is a per-frame fraction of zombies matched (not mean average precision over a full COCO-style protocol).
Distortion slices follow filename tags and \texttt{distortion\_eval\_groups} (\texttt{none}, \texttt{low}, \texttt{high}, \texttt{extreme}, \texttt{mixed}).

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conc}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Under our protocol, \textbf{RT-DETR} and \textbf{YOLOv11n} achieve the highest \texttt{mixed} test precision (Table~\ref{tab:bestmixed}), aligning with the reported accuracy--speed profile of RT-DETR on COCO-like data~\citep{zhao2024rtdetr} and the continued evolution of the YOLO line~\citep{redmon2017yolo9000,ultralytics_yolo_docs} (see also the third-party YOLO11 survey~\citep{khanam2024yolo11}).
  \item \textbf{Heatmap CNNs with manual features} reach strong mixed precision at the heatmap training resolution, supporting RQ3: engineered channels help under combined distortions.
  \item \textbf{Faster R-CNN (torchvision), HOG+SVM, and template matching} score near zero under \texttt{mixed} in this campaign (Table~\ref{tab:bestmixed}); we treat them as negative controls or as requiring a different hyperparameter/label protocol, not as evidence that two-stage or classical methods are inherently unusable on KAZ~\citep{ren2015fasterrcnn,dalal2005hog}.
  \item For \textbf{resource-constrained deployment}, heatmap+manual-feature models trade a small precision gap for predictable CNN cost; YOLO/RT-DETR remain preferable when Ultralytics training and weights are acceptable.
\end{itemize}
